The GASING method is grounded in the core philosophy that all children can learn mathematics if they are given the opportunity to learn with good teachers and the right methods. This philosophy is based on the belief that difficulties in learning mathematics are generally not caused by a lack of cognitive ability, but rather by fear (math anxiety) and the mistaken belief that mathematics is difficult or can only be understood by “smart” children (Foley et al., 2017).

GASING emphasizes that mathematics is not merely a collection of formulas or disconnected topics, but a structured and interconnected system. This approach is in line with research findings showing that mathematics learning which emphasizes the discovery of patterns and structures is more effective in developing deep conceptual understanding (Boaler, 2016). In the GASING process, there are also “critical points” in each mathematical topic that students must pass; once these points are crossed, students no longer experience significant difficulty with the topic. This mastery learning–oriented approach ensures that no concept is skipped before moving on to the next one.

The GASING philosophy also seeks to transform the paradigm of both teachers and students toward mathematics. What was once considered difficult and frightening becomes something enjoyable, closely related to daily life, and positively challenging. This philosophy supports a shift in students’ mindsets, making them more confident, willing to try, and unafraid of making mistakes.

In its implementation, GASING uses a concrete–abstract–mental arithmetic approach. The learning process always begins with concrete elements that students can see, touch, or visualize. For example, the concept of multiplication is not immediately introduced as “3 × 2 = 6,” but instead visualized as three boxes each containing two objects, with the total representing the sum of all those objects.

After the concrete stage, students are guided to the abstract stage, where they begin to use mathematical symbols and notation. This stage helps students understand that symbols are representations of concepts they have already encountered concretely.

The final stage is mental arithmetic, which is one of the main emphases of GASING. At this stage, students are trained to calculate mentally, gradually becoming able to perform arithmetic operations quickly without any tools. Mental arithmetic practice not only improves accuracy and speed of thinking, but also trains balanced coordination between the left brain (logic) and the right brain (visualization and imagination).

In addition, GASING places strong emphasis on a fun learning atmosphere. Learning sessions are filled with songs, movements, and games that are enjoyable yet challenging. For example, to memorize multiplication, students sing special songs; to train fast calculation, they play number games. This joyful atmosphere encourages children to learn without coercion or fear, in line with the principles of being enjoyable and fun. With this effective and cheerful method, Prof. Surya claims that students can master the entire elementary school mathematics curriculum (normally completed in six years) in just six months of intensive learning. The strengths of the GASING method are summarized by Surya into 12 key points, including: it can be learned across ages (children and adults alike), always starts from concrete concepts, enables rapid mastery of basic arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) without tools, fosters creativity and IQ, cultivates independence and discipline (EQ), builds resilience (AQ), improves motor skills, aligns with the school curriculum, is highly time-efficient, and even provides benefits for mental health.
