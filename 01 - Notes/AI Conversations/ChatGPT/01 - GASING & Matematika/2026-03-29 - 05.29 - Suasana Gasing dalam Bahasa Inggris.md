### 🤖 ChatGPT:
**Disposition** artinya **kecenderungan sikap atau kebiasaan batin untuk bertindak atau berpikir dengan cara tertentu**.

Dalam konteks **critical thinking**, disposition bukan sekadar **bisa** berpikir kritis, tetapi **cenderung mau** berpikir kritis.

Jadi bedanya:

- **Skill** = kemampuan
- **Disposition** = kecenderungan untuk memakai kemampuan itu

Contoh:
Seseorang mungkin **mampu** menganalisis argumen dengan baik, tetapi kalau ia **tidak mau** memeriksa fakta, tidak mau mendengar pandangan lain, atau malas berpikir hati-hati, berarti skill ada, tetapi **disposition**-nya lemah.

Dalam critical thinking, disposition bisa mencakup:
- mau mencari kebenaran
- terbuka pada bukti baru
- mau mengoreksi kesalahan
- tidak terburu-buru menyimpulkan
- mau berpikir jernih dan adil

Terjemahan yang paling pas tergantung konteks:
- **kecenderungan**
- **watak berpikir**
- **sikap mental**
- **kecenderungan sikap**

Untuk kalimat akademik, ini bagus:

**Disposition refers to a person’s consistent willingness or tendency to think and act in a particular way.**

Atau dalam konteks berpikir kritis:

**A critical thinking disposition is the tendency to use critical thinking skills habitually and willingly.**

## Revised section text

### Activities That Elicit Critical Thinking

One of the distinctive features of the GASING method is that almost every activity contains a moment of evaluation hidden within play. Students are not explicitly being taught “critical thinking.” They are singing, comparing, correcting, explaining, or detecting mistakes, and within all of that, the capacity to evaluate, question, and improve is being exercised continuously.

The six activities below illustrate the broader range of GASING learning activities through which critical thinking emerges. Some involve concrete verification, others require verbal explanation, error detection, or pattern recognition. Together, they show that critical thinking in GASING is not taught as a separate subject, but cultivated through repeated participation in meaningful learning acts. fileciteturn6file0

### Table 9.4 GASING Learning Activities That Elicit Critical Thinking

| Activity | Intelligence Developed | Why Critical Thinking Emerges |
|---|---|---|
| Finger verification | Bodily-Kinaesthetic + Logical-Mathematical | When the finger count is wrong, the student immediately sees it and corrects it. Fingers function as a concrete verification tool, producing instant feedback without waiting for the teacher. |
| Error analysis | Logical-Mathematical + Intrapersonal | Detecting errors, whether self-made or deliberately planted by the trainer, trains active evaluation and self-reflection rather than passive acceptance. For example, students may be shown 99 + 34 with the tens result written as 13, and must identify and correct the mistake. |
| “Why” questions | Linguistic + Logical-Mathematical | Students are invited to ask why an answer is correct and how it can be proven, building the habit of questioning rather than merely accepting. |
| Multiplication pattern discovery | Naturalist + Logical-Mathematical | Students are presented with an unordered set of multiples and asked to identify what they share and extend the pattern. This trains them to detect hidden order, form a hypothesis, and verify it. |
| Multiplication song with wrong note | Musical + Logical-Mathematical | Each multiplication result is linked to a distinct melody note. When the teacher deliberately sings the wrong note, students immediately detect the mismatch. The response shows that evaluation is already taking place through internalised pattern recognition. |
| Student explanation of steps | Linguistic + Intrapersonal + Logical-Mathematical | When students explain how they arrived at an answer, they must organise their reasoning, monitor their thinking, and make their logic visible to others. This turns understanding into something that can be examined and improved. |

Among GASING’s many activities, some take the form of routines, explanation tasks, songs, or error-detection exercises, while others take the more explicit form of games. In all of them, what appears on the surface to be play or participation conceals a genuine cognitive demand: to calculate, verify, compare, decide, and correct.

The following table narrows the focus to three of GASING’s principal games. These deserve separate attention because they intensify critical thinking through speed, interaction, competition, and immediate feedback. fileciteturn6file0

### Table 9.5 GASING Games That Elicit Critical Thinking

| Game | Intelligence Developed | How Critical Thinking Emerges |
|---|---|---|
| Suit Jumlah / Suit Kali (Finger Arithmetic) | Logical-Mathematical + Interpersonal + Bodily-Kinaesthetic | Two students simultaneously reveal a number of fingers and race to call out the total or product. Each must calculate quickly while cross-verifying the opponent’s answer. Critical evaluation emerges naturally through the game dynamic. |
| Pasangan Jari (GASING Greeting) | Bodily-Kinaesthetic + Logical-Mathematical + Intrapersonal | The teacher shows 2 fingers while saying “GASING Greeting five”; the student must immediately show 3 fingers. Performed at high speed, the game compels the learner to process, verify, and respond within seconds. |
| Jari Hilang (Missing Fingers) | Logical-Mathematical + Bodily-Kinaesthetic + Intrapersonal | The teacher shows 8 fingers with some bent, and students infer how many are hidden. This builds the habit of concrete reasoning and early algebraic thinking through visible evidence rather than guesswork. |

All three games share one important characteristic: there is no room for passivity. In Suit Jumlah, a wrong answer is immediately visible to the opponent. In GASING Greeting, slow or incorrect thinking immediately affects the response. In Missing Fingers, an unsupported answer is easily challenged by concrete evidence.

Each game builds an evaluative reflex, the habit of checking before concluding, which gradually becomes a settled mode of thinking long after the game has ended.

The transformation in students is rarely abrupt. It unfolds in stages, from an initially passive mindset dependent on teacher confirmation toward an active one capable of independent verification. fileciteturn6file0
