### **ABSTRACT**

The **GASING Method** (an acronym for *Easy, Fun, and Enjoyable*) was developed by Prof. Yohanes Surya, Ph.D. as an innovative approach to addressing Indonesia’s numeracy crisis, particularly in **3T regions** (*Frontier, Outermost, and Disadvantaged areas*). This method emphasizes a structured learning progression from **Concrete → Abstract → Mental Arithmetic**, implemented within an active and joyful learning environment grounded in a **Pedagogy of Love**.

From a theoretical perspective, GASING represents a point of convergence of **eleven major educational theories**: Behaviorism, Cognitivism, Constructivism, Multiple Intelligences, Humanistic Psychology, Educational Neuroscience, Experiential Learning, Motivation Theory, Sociocultural Theory, 21st-Century Skills (8C), and the Pedagogy of Love. This holistic integration explains why GASING achieves effectiveness far exceeding that of single-theory-based interventions.

This white paper presents an empirical analysis of GASING implementations involving **23,961 participants** (15,426 students and 8,535 teachers) across **35 provinces and 144 districts/cities** throughout Indonesia. Evaluation results demonstrate:

- An average student improvement of **4.29×** (from 17.6 to 76.2)

### **A. The Math Anxiety Pathway: A Negative Cascade**

🧠 **NEUROSCIENCE CONCEPT:** *Amygdala → Cortisol → Disrupted Prefrontal Cortex*

The math anxiety pathway begins when mathematical stimuli—problems, tests, or even the mere sight of numbers—are **perceived as a threat** by the brain. The **amygdala**, a brain structure that functions as a *“threat detector,”* becomes activated and triggers a **fight-or-flight response**. This activation stimulates the release of **cortisol**, the stress hormone that prepares the body to respond to physical danger.

In a learning context, however, cortisol is **counterproductive**. It disrupts the function of the **prefrontal cortex**—the brain region responsible for logical reasoning, planning, and problem-solving. When the prefrontal cortex is impaired, **working memory**—the cognitive capacity to store and manipulate information simultaneously—becomes inhibited.

---

### ⚠️ **NEGATIVE CASCADE OF MATH ANXIETY**

1. **MATHEMATICAL STIMULUS** is perceived as a **THREAT** by the brain  
2. **AMYGDALA** (threat detector) is activated → fight-or-flight response  
3. **CORTISOL** (stress hormone) is released throughout the body  
4. Cortisol **DISRUPTS** **PREFRONTAL CORTEX** function  
5. **WORKING MEMORY** becomes **INHIBITED**  
6. The student experiences a **“BLANK” state** — inability to think clearly  
7. The belief *“I can’t do math”* is **CONFIRMED** → **SELF-FULFILLING PROPHECY**

As a result, students are unable to think clearly, experience a “blank” mental state, and further reinforce the belief that they are *“bad at mathematics.”* This is a **self-fulfilling prophecy** driven by neurobiological mechanisms. The more frequently students experience failure due to math anxiety, the stronger the amygdala response becomes to mathematical stimuli in the future—creating a **vicious cycle** that is difficult to break.

5.3.2 Hebbian Learning: "Neurons that Fire Together, Wire Together"
Prinsip Dasar Hebbian Learning
Donald Hebb (1949) dalam bukunya "The Organization of Behavior" memformulasikan prinsip yang kini dikenal sebagai Hebbian Learning:

"When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A's efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased."
— Donald Hebb (1949)

Prinsip ini disederhanakan menjadi aforisme yang terkenal: "Neurons that fire together, wire together" (neuron yang menyala bersama akan terhubung bersama). Setiap kali dua neuron teraktivasi secara simultan, koneksi sinaptik di antara mereka menjadi lebih kuat.
🧠 KONSEP NEUROSAINS: Neurons that Fire Together, Wire Together — Koneksi diperkuat oleh aktivasi simultan

## **5.3.2 Hebbian Learning: “Neurons That Fire Together, Wire Together”**

### **Core Principle of Hebbian Learning**

Donald Hebb (1949), in *The Organization of Behavior*, formulated what is now known as **Hebbian Learning**:

> *“When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A’s efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased.”*  
— Donald Hebb (1949)

This principle is commonly summarized in the well-known aphorism: **“Neurons that fire together, wire together.”** Each time two neurons are activated simultaneously, the synaptic connection between them becomes stronger.

🧠 **NEUROSCIENCE CONCEPT:** *Neurons that fire together, wire together — connections are strengthened through co-activation*

---

### **Synaptic Strengthening in GASING**

Synaptic strengthening refers to the process by which synapses (connections between neurons) become stronger when repeatedly activated. GASING optimizes synaptic strengthening through **repetitive practice embedded in positive emotional contexts**.

This positive context is crucial. Research indicates that dopamine released during pleasurable experiences acts as a **reinforcement signal** that accelerates synaptic consolidation. The same repetition under stress or fear can instead strengthen negative associations.

## **5.3.3 Long-Term Potentiation (LTP): The Cellular Mechanism of Memory Formation**

### **What Is Long-Term Potentiation?**

**Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)** is a cellular mechanism underlying learning and memory formation. First described by **Bliss & Lømo (1973)**, LTP refers to the persistent strengthening of synaptic connections following repeated high-frequency stimulation.

Simply put: when neuron A repeatedly and strongly activates neuron B, the connection between them becomes permanently more efficient. The same signal in the future will pass from A to B more easily.

### **Table 5.34. Characteristics of Long-Term Potentiation and Relevance to GASING**

| LTP Characteristic | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Cooperativity** | LTP requires simultaneous activation of multiple synaptic inputs—GASING activates multiple pathways concurrently |
| **Associativity** | Weak inputs can undergo LTP when paired with strong inputs—music/emotion strengthens mathematical facts |
| **Input specificity** | LTP is specific to activated synapses—it does not spread randomly |
| **Persistence** | LTP can last days, weeks, or a lifetime—foundation of long-term memory |

*Source: LTP characteristics (Bliss & Lømo, 1973)*

---

### **Molecular Mechanisms of LTP**

At the molecular level, LTP involves key steps:

1. **NMDA receptor activation:** repeated stimulation opens NMDA “gates,” allowing calcium ions into the postsynaptic neuron  
2. **Calcium cascade:** calcium activates enzymes (kinases) that modify synaptic proteins  
3. **AMPA receptor insertion:** more AMPA receptors are inserted into the postsynaptic membrane, increasing sensitivity  
4. **Structural change:** over time, dendritic spine growth and synaptic structural strengthening occur  
5. **Protein synthesis:** gene activation produces proteins that stabilize synaptic changes long-term
