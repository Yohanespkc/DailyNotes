**4. Why Does This Method Work So Well?**

Many people ask what actually makes this method successful.

**a. Learning is taught through the concept of “seeing, hearing, speaking, and moving.”**

At the very first stage of teaching numbers, we use fingers.  
We show one finger while saying “one.”  
Then we show two fingers while saying “two,” and so on, up to five fingers.

Next, we show two fingers—the index finger and the middle finger—and ask the children to follow our finger movements while saying “two.”  
Then we show two fingers again, this time the middle finger and the ring finger, and we again say “two.”  
Next, we use the left index finger and the right index finger and also say “two.”

We repeat this process many times for all numbers below five.

As a remarkable result, students are then able to perform additions such as 1 + 3, 2 + 3, 1 + 2, and so on, for sums below five.

For numbers 6–10, we apply the same approach.  
Then, to learn that 6 = 5 + 1, 4 + 2, or 3 + 3, we use games that we designed ourselves using many cards. This is how we practice addition for numbers from 6 to 10.

After that, to master addition from 11 to 20, we use a variety of card games.

Only after these stages do we introduce fast mental calculation.

As a result, all additions can be performed quickly.
