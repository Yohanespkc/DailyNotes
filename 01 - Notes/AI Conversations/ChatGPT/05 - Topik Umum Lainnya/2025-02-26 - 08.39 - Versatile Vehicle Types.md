Versatile vehicles refer to types of transportation that can adapt to a variety of environments and tasks. These vehicles are designed for multi-purpose use, often combining features of different vehicle types to provide flexibility in their function.

Some examples of versatile vehicles include:

1. **All-terrain vehicles (ATVs)**: Built to handle rough, uneven terrains, often used in outdoor activities, agriculture, or military applications.
  
2. **Utility vehicles (UTVs)**: Known for their ability to navigate off-road environments, often used for work on farms, construction sites, or recreational areas.

3. **SUVs (Sport Utility Vehicles)**: Known for being able to transition between city roads and more rugged, off-road paths. They offer the versatility of both urban and adventure-friendly capabilities.

4. **Crossover vehicles**: Combining the characteristics of cars and SUVs, they offer good fuel efficiency with enough space and capability for light off-roading.

5. **Electric vehicles (EVs)** with off-road capability: As EV technology evolves, more electric cars, like the Tesla Cybertruck, are designed to offer both versatility and eco-friendliness.

6. **Motorhomes/RVs (Recreational Vehicles)**: Vehicles designed for both travel and living, giving users the flexibility to work, sleep, and travel all in one.

7. **Amphibious vehicles**: Vehicles that can travel both on land and water, offering an extreme level of versatility, often used in military, exploration, or unique transportation needs.
