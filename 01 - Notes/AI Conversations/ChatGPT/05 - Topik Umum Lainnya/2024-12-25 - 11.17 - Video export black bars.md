# 💬 Video export black bars

**Tanggal:** `2024-12-25 11.17`  
**Sumber:** ChatGPT Export  

---

### 👤 User:
in final cut pro the video suddenly filled all the screen so it made the video zoomed in, so i transformed it to zoom out but when i exported the video why did the video become so small, and so many black space around it

### 🤖 ChatGPT:
The issue you described likely happened because of the mismatch between your project's **aspect ratio** or **resolution** and the **export settings**. Here's why this happens and how to fix it:

### Why This Happened:
1. **Aspect Ratio Mismatch:**
   - If the original video clip and your project's aspect ratio are different (e.g., the video is 4:3 but the project is set to 16:9), Final Cut Pro will try to fill the screen, causing the video to zoom in.
   - When you adjusted the video to "zoom out" using the Transform tool, the video scaled down to fit within the project's aspect ratio, which then created black bars around the video on export.

2. **Export Settings Issue:**
   - If the export resolution or aspect ratio doesn't match your project settings, it can introduce additional black bars or scaling issues.

---

### How to Fix It:

#### 1. Check and Match Your Project Settings:
   - Go to **Project Properties** by selecting the project in the Timeline, then press **Command + J** and click **Modify Settings**.
   - Check the **Resolution** (e.g., 1920x1080 for 16:9) and **Aspect Ratio**. Make sure it matches the original video's aspect ratio or your desired output.

#### 2. Adjust Clip Scaling Properly:
   - Select the video clip in the Timeline.
   - Open the **Inspector** (press **Command + 4**) and look at the **Scale** and **Position** settings.
   - Use the **"Fit"** or **"Fill"** options in the Transform settings to resize the clip correctly:
     - **Fit:** Ensures the entire video fits inside the frame but may create black bars if the aspect ratios differ.
     - **Fill:** Fills the entire frame but crops parts of the video that don't fit the aspect ratio.

#### 3. Export with the Correct Settings:
   - When exporting, go to **File > Share > Master File (Default)** and click **Settings**.
   - Make sure the **Resolution** matches your project's settings (e.g., 1920x1080 or 3840x2160).
   - Set **Format** to **Video and Audio** and ensure the codec and quality match your desired output.

---

### Tips to Avoid This in the Future:
- If you want to avoid black bars, consider using the **Crop tool** to adjust your video's framing manually instead of scaling down too much.
- Ensure your project's resolution and aspect ratio are set correctly before starting the edit.
- Use consistent resolution/aspect ratio across all clips if possible.

### 👤 User:
WHATS DIFFERENCE BETWEEN EXPORT FILE NORMAL AND ALSO SOCIAL PLATFORM EXPORT IN FINAL CUT PRO

### 🤖 ChatGPT:
The difference between **"Export File (Default)"** and **social media platform-specific exports** in Final Cut Pro lies in the settings optimized for the intended purpose of the video. Here's a breakdown:

---

### **1. Export File (Default)**
This is the most general-purpose export option.

#### **Characteristics:**
- **Resolution & Aspect Ratio:**
  - Matches the resolution and aspect ratio of your project settings.
  - Allows you to export in a high-resolution format like **1080p** or **4K**.

- **File Format:**
  - Exports as a standard video file (commonly in **QuickTime (.mov)** or **MP4** formats).
  - Offers high-quality formats like **ProRes** for editing purposes or smaller formats like H.264 for playback.

- **Customizable Settings:**
  - You can manually adjust the codec, resolution, and quality.
  - Useful for a wide range of purposes: archival, professional distribution, further editing, or uploading to any platform.

#### **Use Cases:**
- Archiving your project in the highest possible quality.
- Sharing the video on multiple platforms manually.
- Further processing the video in other software.

---

### **2. Social Platform Export**
This is tailored for specific social media platforms (e.g., YouTube, Facebook, Instagram).

#### **Characteristics:**
- **Pre-Configured Settings:**
  - Optimized for the requirements of specific platforms (e.g., YouTube prefers H.264 codec, 16:9 aspect ratio, and high bitrate).
  - Automatically adjusts aspect ratios for platforms like Instagram (e.g., 1:1 for square or 9:16 for stories/reels).

- **File Format:**
  - Usually exports in the **H.264** codec for smaller file sizes and faster uploads.
  - Export settings ensure compatibility with social media platforms without manual adjustments.

- **Resolution & Frame Rate:**
  - Resolution and frame rate are aligned with the platform's preferred standards (e.g., 1080p at 30fps for YouTube or 720p for Instagram Stories).

- **Direct Upload Option:**
  - Final Cut Pro offers the ability to upload directly to platforms like YouTube or Facebook (if linked to your account) after exporting.

#### **Use Cases:**
- Exporting directly for social media distribution.
- Optimized settings to ensure videos look good on specific platforms.
- Quick and hassle-free upload workflows.

---

### **Key Differences:**

| Feature                 | Export File (Default)                | Social Platform Export              |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Customization**       | Fully customizable                  | Pre-set for specific platforms      |
| **File Size**           | Larger (depending on quality)       | Smaller (optimized for web upload) |
| **Formats**             | ProRes, H.264, HEVC, etc.           | Primarily H.264 or H.265 (smaller size) |
| **Aspect Ratio**        | Matches project settings            | Adjusted for platform (e.g., 16:9, 1:1, 9:16) |
| **Direct Upload**       | Manual upload after export          | Option for direct upload to platforms |
| **Quality**             | High (for archival or professional use) | Lower (optimized for social media compression) |

---

### **Which Should You Use?**
- Use **Export File (Default)**:
  - When you need full control over quality, resolution, and codec.
  - For professional or high-quality archival purposes.
  - If you're editing further in other software.
  
- Use **Social Platform Export**:
  - When you're exporting content specifically for platforms like YouTube, Instagram, or Facebook.
  - To save time with platform-optimized settings and direct upload options.
  - When file size and quick uploads are more important than maximum quality.
